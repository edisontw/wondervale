## 1. Story purpose and episode promise

**Why this episode exists:** The old map helped the trio understand change, but cannot tell them how to use the shared courtyard today. Two present-day needs compete for the same small space; an apparently elegant direct path would cut through a quiet area or ignore a genuine safety boundary. Milo must test and revise a proposal with Nia and Finn, not vote on a correct answer. The Common is reopened for current people **only after** a workable, approved plan is tested; the old North Hill segment stays closed.

**Opening trigger:** Two E10 needs are displayed on an unfinished, source-qualified needs board. The trio finds that a quick drawn line occupies the same patch as one proposed use. The child is invited to try a *temporary*, reversible floor layout rather than approve a finished design.

**End state:** A tested compromise serves both represented needs within declared constraints, a **new present-day loop** (not the old route) is visible on an old/new map spread, and earlier world changes remain visible. No forced perfect consensus, magic bypass, villain, treasure or Season 2 emergency.

## 4. Learning, EFL and optional parent prompt

**Two primary goals (locked weave):** (1) synthesize earlier evidence and multiple physical/human constraints into a workable shared-space and route plan; (2) compare alternatives, explain a tradeoff and revise after a meaningful test. **Secondary:** represent past and present accurately in the Chronicle.

**Evidence of thinking:** at least two tested layouts; one conflict identified through a visible trial; one revision preserving both needs; an earlier discovery cited (E03 delivery, E06 drainage, E07 user impact, E09 closure, or E10 needs); a safe connected present-day loop, not old-route tracing.

**EFL communicative functions:** `We could…`, `This way is for…`, `It blocks…`, `Both need…`, `Let's test.`, `We changed it because…`, `The old way was… / Now we…`. Use icon + short text and optional read-aloud, never a language test. A child can reason with spatial pieces and picture cues without typing or fluent reading.

**Parent cue (optional, after first failed trial):** “Who could use this path? Who might find it hard?” **Final reflection (optional):** “Which earlier discovery changed your plan?” Parents do not position pieces, choose a winner or tell the child the route. Child-alone prompts must remain available.

**Misconceptions to surface gently:** the shortest line is best; a compromise means nobody benefits; a historic route should be restored exactly; a good final design never needs revision. Failure reveals a specific collision or unmet need and offers immediate retry, not points or punishment.
