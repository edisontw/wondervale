## 2. Episode contract

- **Purpose:** give Finn his major constructive mistake-and-revision episode. Being clever is useful; testing for more than one person and purpose makes an idea better.
- **Opening trigger:** after **verified canonical E06 completion**, Finn shows Milo and Nia a compact connector mock-up from the two garden areas toward the Lantern Steps approach. It appears to shorten their walk. The player must **try it** rather than being told it is wrong.
- **Problem:** the first arrangement works for Milo but obstructs a second legitimate user and/or interrupts a visible drainage or routine-access function. “Shortest” and “best for everyone” are not identical.
- **Local stakes:** a resident still needs a usable route, and the garden's water path cannot be treated as spare space. The trio can test and revise without endangering people or altering unapproved terrain.
- **Turning point:** Milo's successful walk is only the **first test**. A second-user trial and a water/access check reveal an actual, visually observable conflict. Finn says the idea needs changing, without being shamed.
- **Resolution:** the child moves/reconfigures connector pieces in a bounded prototype, runs both-user and water/access tests again, and confirms a design that preserves these functions. The storybook outcome is a **proposed** revised connector; production may show a real persistent path only after site/art/permission approval.
- **World consequence (proposed, not activated):** a revised connector is visible on later visits and leads to the **base** of Lantern Steps; it does not open the Steps or North Hill. Chronicle keeps a first/changed layout comparison and Finn's “who else?” realization, not a score.
- **E08 hook:** at the Steps' base the trio notices a partial lighting pattern; the **evening viewpoint test belongs to E08**, not E07.
- **Non-goals:** a route-selection quiz, E03 delivery replay, forced wrong answer, villainizing Finn or the other user, building an unsafe bypass, inventing real engineering measurements, promising a safe route without physical review, moving E06's garden, revealing the Common, starting E08's lighting solution, timing/scoring the child, or requiring audio.

## 5. Substantial child interactions

### `s01e07-int01` — Walk and notice

- **Story reason:** Finn's shortest route looks useful.
- **Cognitive action:** predict which functions a connector must preserve; distinguish a visible feature from an assumption.
- **Player action:** direct Milo to inspect two or more spatial anchors (entry/turn, shared crossing, water trace) and walk the first model route.
- **Available evidence:** visibly labeled floor/footpath and model channel; no hidden S/N-like imaginary pavement.
- **Feedback:** Milo reaches the end on the model, but the message is **“Works for Milo”**, never “Perfect/complete”.
- **Recovery:** return and inspect again without losing progress; no collision trap.
- **Access:** large touch targets and keyboard route stepping, text alternative to animated walk.
- **Evidence:** child has seen a successful first-user trial and inspected constraints.

### `s01e07-int02` — Test who else uses it

- **Story reason:** the shared passage has other legitimate uses.
- **Cognitive action:** transfer the E03 “works for whom?” question to an engineering prototype.
- **Player action:** start a clearly labeled other-user route test and a separate water/access test; compare traces to Milo's.
- **Feedback:** another user visibly waits/detours at an obstruction **or** a water trace meets the connector, depending on Gate 1 evidence. The authoring prototype can demonstrate **both** conflicts using obviously synthetic geometry; this is not evidence of the real site.
- **Recovery:** tests can be repeated, paused, stepped through and reviewed; no one is harmed or blamed.
- **Access:** captions and spatial highlights independent of sound/color; keyboard/touch parity.
- **Evidence:** child can see why one successful trip is not a full solution.

### `s01e07-int03` — Move, try, improve

- **Story reason:** Finn asks Milo to help revise rather than defend his first idea.
- **Cognitive action:** balance route convenience, another user's passage and water/access function.
- **Player action:** relocate/reorient one or two **removable model pieces** on illustrated snap points; run all trials again. Avoid precision drag; snap/tap/keyboard are equivalent.
- **Information:** before each test, show proposed connector footprint, shared route and channel; all constraints inspectable.
- **Valid strategies:** a child may choose a good revision first or explore several; no forced failure or secret “correct” option. If a model arrangement does not satisfy constraints, the exact overlapping feature is shown and can be changed.
- **Feedback:** three distinct trace outcomes (Milo, other user, water/access), plus a before/after overlay. A clear design enables a **provisional** completion; never a physical safety certificate.
- **Recovery:** undo/retry/reset the model without resetting story progress or writing a save.
- **Access:** keyboard focus order, large tap targets, reduced-motion step-by-step trace, Listen/caption fallback.
- **Evidence:** one tested revised layout preserves more than one user's/function's needs and the child can indicate what changed.

## 6. Learning, EFL and parent

**Primary (from approved weave):** (1) engineering test → side-effect identification → revision; (2) systems/perspective reasoning across multiple users and functions. **Secondary:** spatial fit/comparison only as the interaction needs it. **Transfer:** E03 delivery route limits, E06 runoff and shared garden path. **Misconception:** a design is done once it works for its intended traveler; a side effect can be ignored; sunk effort makes a plan unchangeable. **Evidence of thinking:** child tests both first and revised layouts against **multiple constraints**, not merely chooses a “better” label.

**EFL communicative functions:** describe `too narrow / blocks / gets wet`, compare `first / now / better`, propose `move / change / try another way`, explain `because / so`. Draft short reusable lines: “It works for me.” “Can they pass?” “The path is blocked.” “Water needs a way.” “Let's move this.” “Now both can pass.” “Better for whom?” Use visible subjects, simple syntax and optional Listen/captions; **do not simplify away multi-user causal reasoning**. Do not require children to say “drainage” or exact measurements.

**Optional parent cue `s01e07-pc01`**, shown only **after** Milo's first successful trial and before redesign: “It works for us. Who else uses this place?” Follow-up after test: “What changed, and why?” Adult must not choose a snap point or tell the child the answer. Child-alone path remains complete.

**Scaffolding:** (1) free try, (2) highlight the user/path/channel that was not tested, (3) show how to activate a test, (4) compare two traces, (5) nonspoiling hint to move a piece. No automatic redesign, mandatory speech, timer or penalty. All meaningful results have text and shape cues, not color/audio alone.
