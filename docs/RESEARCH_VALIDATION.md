# Research & Validation Plan

## Principle

A technically complete episode is not validated until actual parent-child pairs can use it with minimal intervention.

## What to validate

Do not measure only completion.

Evaluate at least:

### Child experience
- understands what is happening;
- knows what can be interacted with;
- wants to explore;
- shows curiosity;
- tolerates mistakes;
- remembers characters/events;
- wants another episode.

### Parent experience
- understands their role quickly;
- prompts feel natural rather than instructional;
- prompts create conversation;
- parent does not take over;
- parent does not need specialist knowledge;
- session length feels manageable.

### Learning behavior
Look for evidence such as:

- prediction before action;
- noticing relevant evidence;
- revising an idea;
- explaining a choice;
- transferring an earlier idea;
- asking a new question.

### Interaction quality
Observe:

- who holds or controls the device;
- whether they talk to each other or only to the screen;
- whether prompts interrupt flow;
- whether the child waits for adult approval;
- whether the adult reads too much;
- whether instructions are misunderstood.

## Initial testing groups

Test the vertical slice with different profiles, including:

- ages 6–7;
- ages 8–9;
- stronger and weaker readers;
- families comfortable and less comfortable with English where relevant;
- different levels of adult participation.

The purpose is not statistical proof at the prototype stage. It is to reveal design failure modes before content production scales.

## Episode 01 exit questions

Before using E01 as the template for later episodes:

1. Did the story motivate the interaction?
2. Did the adult-child exchange actually occur?
3. Was the learning goal visible through behavior?
4. Was any instruction confusing?
5. Did failure support thinking?
6. Was there a memorable character/world moment?
7. Did the ending create interest in what comes next?
8. Did either player feel rushed or overloaded?
9. Which moments should be shortened, removed, or made more open?
10. Would this format still feel fresh after ten episodes?

## Content-level longitudinal checks

Across several episodes, inspect:

- repeated mechanics;
- repeated parent prompts;
- repeated story structures;
- neglected skill families;
- concepts introduced but never revisited;
- unresolved story promises;
- character growth that is too fast or inconsistent;
- world changes that are mentioned but not visible.

## Instrumentation

The MVP should prefer observation and optional parent feedback over invasive telemetry.

If analytics are later added, define a specific research question before collecting each event.
