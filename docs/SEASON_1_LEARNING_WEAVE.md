# Season 1 Learning Weave

## Status

**Approved learning architecture for Season 1.**

This document maps learning onto the locked story in [SEASON_1_ARCHITECTURE.md](SEASON_1_ARCHITECTURE.md).

It locks:

- the major Season 1 learning threads;
- each Core Episode's primary/secondary learning role;
- revisit and transfer relationships;
- high-level EFL language functions;
- important misconceptions/incomplete models to design for;
- meaningful evidence of thinking;
- one high-value parent discussion opportunity per episode.

It does **not** lock:

- exact activity mechanics;
- exact numbers, measurements, reading level, or quantitative difficulty;
- exact vocabulary lists or sentence-length limits;
- final parent-cue wording/timing;
- hints/scaffolds;
- assessment scoring;
- detailed dialogue or scripts.

Those belong to later production stages.

# Design principles

## Story remains upstream

Learning must solve a story problem that already has a dramatic reason to exist.

Do not add a learning task merely because a subject area has not appeared recently.

## Few connected threads, not many isolated objectives

Season 1 deliberately uses a small number of ideas repeatedly in changing contexts.

The intended cycle is:

**Encounter -> Explore -> Understand -> Apply -> Revisit -> Transfer -> Deepen**

A revisit must change purpose, context, representation, or required reasoning.

## EFL is a language-access layer, not the cognitive ceiling

The child may reason about uncertainty, evidence, fairness, systems, and tradeoffs using simple English.

Language support must not give away the thinking.

## Evidence is embedded in action and conversation

Evidence of learning may be visible in:

- what the child notices;
- what the child predicts;
- which route or design the child chooses;
- what evidence the child uses;
- whether the child revises;
- how the child explains;
- whether an earlier idea is used in a new context.

Not all of this should be automatically scored.

# Season 1 learning threads

## Thread A — Evidence, explanation, and revision

Core idea:

**What we notice is not always the same as what we think happened. Good explanations can change when new evidence appears.**

Progression:

- **E01 Encounter:** distinguish observation from first interpretation.
- **E02 Explore:** notice evidence that does not fit a simple explanation.
- **E04 Understand:** compare different kinds of evidence without forcing one immediate answer.
- **E05 Apply:** design a small reversible test that can produce useful evidence.
- **E08 Revisit:** test whether a visual pattern is real rather than accepting one striking observation.
- **E09 Transfer:** reason from limited safe evidence and state what remains unknown.
- **E11 Deepen:** use evidence from many earlier episodes to justify a new plan.

This is the main reasoning spine of Season 1.

## Thread B — Maps, routes, and spatial constraints

Core idea:

**A map represents a place; a route must work in the real world, not only look good on paper.**

Progression:

- **E01 Encounter:** interpret simple map/marker relationships.
- **E02 Explore:** compare mapped route with changed physical space.
- **E03 Apply:** choose routes under different movement/access constraints.
- **E07 Revisit:** design a connector that must fit physical and human constraints.
- **E09 Transfer:** plan an alternate route when the historical route cannot be used.
- **E11 Deepen:** synthesize a current route through a changed town.

Exact scale, distance, measurement units, and numerical complexity remain open for episode production.

## Thread C — Time, sources, and change

Core idea:

**Places change over time, and different records or memories may describe different real moments.**

Progression:

- **E03 Encounter:** hear two conflicting route memories.
- **E04 Explore/Understand:** sequence evidence and reconcile sources through chronology.
- **E05 Apply:** use objects and records to infer an earlier system.
- **E06 Revisit:** understand a moved garden as continuity through change rather than simple loss.
- **E10 Transfer:** infer several past uses of the Common from mixed evidence.
- **E11 Deepen:** represent both past and present without treating one as the only correct version.

## Thread D — Design, systems, constraints, and iteration

Core idea:

**A solution can work in one way and still cause another problem. Good design considers constraints, users, side effects, testing, and revision.**

Progression:

- **E03 Encounter:** shortest route is not best for every delivery.
- **E06 Explore:** site conditions help explain why an earlier arrangement changed.
- **E07 Understand/Apply:** a successful prototype creates side effects and must be redesigned.
- **E09 Revisit:** a good solution may be an alternate route rather than defeating a constraint.
- **E10 Transfer:** identify what a shared space needs to do for several kinds of users.
- **E11 Deepen:** balance multiple functions and constraints in one final design.

## Thread E — Perspective, community needs, and tradeoffs

Core idea:

**Different people can have different valid needs or memories; a shared solution requires understanding more than one perspective.**

Progression:

- **E03 Encounter:** different users need different routes.
- **E04 Explore:** different memories can both be sincere and partly/fully valid.
- **E06 Apply:** preservation and adaptation can both matter.
- **E07 Revisit:** a design that is convenient for the trio can burden someone else.
- **E10 Transfer:** gather current needs before deciding what the Common should become.
- **E11 Deepen:** choose and justify a workable compromise without pretending every preference can be fully satisfied.

# EFL functional progression

Season 1 EFL planning is based on **language functions**, not isolated vocabulary lists.

Exact language forms remain open until episode production.

## Early season — notice and locate

Natural functions:

- describe visible features;
- say where something is;
- ask simple location questions;
- compare two visible things;
- express uncertainty: maybe / I think / I'm not sure.

## Middle season — sequence, predict, test, explain

Natural functions:

- before / after / then / now;
- give and follow directions;
- predict what may happen;
- give a reason;
- describe a test;
- compare first try / second try;
- explain what changed.

## Late season — alternatives, needs, and justification

Natural functions:

- suggest another way;
- state a constraint or need;
- agree/disagree simply and respectfully;
- compare alternatives;
- explain a choice;
- acknowledge uncertainty;
- retell how an idea changed.

Useful language should recur in new purposes rather than becoming a drill.

# Episode learning map

## S01E01 — The Map Behind the Board

### Primary learning goals

1. **Reasoning:** separate observation from interpretation.
2. **Spatial skill:** interpret a simple map/marker as a representation of a real place.

### Secondary learning

Describe and compare visible details.

### Revisit / transfer role

First encounter for Threads A and B.

### EFL language functions

- describing: “I see…”
- locating: here / there / next to / behind
- uncertainty: maybe / I think

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- every unusual mark must be an intentional clue;
- a map must match the current world exactly;
- the first interesting explanation is probably correct.

### Evidence of thinking

The child can point to or describe an observed feature separately from a guess about what it means.

### Parent discussion opportunity

Before interpreting the map: **What can we notice before we decide what it means?**

This is a discussion intent, not locked final cue wording.

## S01E02 — The Bridge That Stops

### Primary learning goals

1. **Reasoning:** revise an explanation when evidence does not fit.
2. **Spatial reasoning:** compare a mapped connection with present physical space.

### Secondary learning

Cause/effect reasoning with uncertainty.

### Revisit / transfer role

Revisits E01 observation-versus-interpretation and applies it to environmental evidence.

### EFL language functions

- compare: same / different
- describe change: was / is / changed
- simple reasons: because
- uncertainty about causes

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- one visible environmental change explains everything;
- a barrier is automatically a puzzle the player should bypass;
- if an old map differs from today, one of them must be “wrong.”

### Evidence of thinking

The child notices at least one feature that weakens the first explanation and adjusts the explanation or keeps alternatives open.

### Parent discussion opportunity

**What does not fit our first idea?**

## S01E03 — Three Deliveries, One Wrong Turn

### Primary learning goals

1. **Spatial/mathematical reasoning:** plan and compare routes under constraints.
2. **Perspective/design reasoning:** recognize that different users may need different workable routes.

### Secondary learning

Informal estimation of shorter/longer, easier/harder, narrow/wide, stairs/level access.

### Revisit / transfer role

Transfers map/route thinking from mystery exploration into a practical current-town problem.

Begins Threads D and E.

### EFL language functions

- directions: go / turn / up / down / past
- comparison: shorter / longer / easier
- reasons: “This way works because…”

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- shortest route is always best;
- one good route should work for everyone;
- efficiency has only one dimension.

### Evidence of thinking

The child changes route choice when the delivery/load constraint changes and can indicate why.

### Parent discussion opportunity

**Which route works for this delivery, and what makes it different from the others?**

## S01E04 — Everyone Remembers It Differently

### Primary learning goals

1. **Humanities skill:** sequence evidence in time.
2. **Reasoning:** compare sources and reconcile apparently conflicting accounts.

### Secondary learning

Perspective taking and uncertainty.

### Revisit / transfer role

Deepens Thread A and establishes Thread C.

Revisits the two route memories from E03.

### EFL language functions

- before / after / first / later
- then / now
- both / different
- “Maybe this was earlier.”

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- if two memories conflict, one person must be lying or completely wrong;
- newest information is always the most accurate for every question;
- there must be one timeless “correct” map.

### Evidence of thinking

The child can place evidence into a plausible order and use time to explain why two accounts may both be valid.

### Parent discussion opportunity

**Could both people be right? What would have to be different?**

## S01E05 — The Station with No Trains

### Primary learning goals

1. **Reasoning:** infer possible function from physical evidence and records.
2. **Scientific/engineering strategy:** design or choose a small reversible test.

### Secondary learning

Reading visual/text evidence for meaning rather than collecting facts.

### Revisit / transfer role

Applies Thread A in a built environment and Thread C to artifacts/records.

### EFL language functions

- prediction: “I think this was for…”
- test language: try / check / if / then
- evidence language: “This mark shows…”

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- an unfamiliar old object has only one plausible function;
- one successful test proves the entire explanation;
- an adult caretaker should simply know and tell the answer.

### Evidence of thinking

The child proposes/chooses a safe test that distinguishes possibilities and updates an idea based on the result.

### Parent discussion opportunity

**What could we try that would teach us something without changing the station permanently?**

## S01E06 — The Garden That Moved

### Primary learning goals

1. **Systems/cause reasoning:** connect site conditions such as drainage/terrain with why a place may change.
2. **Humanities reasoning:** distinguish preservation from exact restoration.

### Secondary learning

Living-system needs and change over time.

### Revisit / transfer role

Revisits Thread C and expands Thread D.

Prepares terrain/drainage reasoning for E07 and E09.

### EFL language functions

- then / now
- move / grow / wet / dry / higher / lower
- cause: because / so
- explaining change

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- if something moved, the old version was “lost” or somebody made a mistake;
- restoring the past exactly is always the respectful choice;
- one factor alone necessarily explains the move.

### Evidence of thinking

The child can give a story-relevant reason why the garden move may have helped and can support a connection rather than a full restoration.

### Parent discussion opportunity

**What might happen if we moved the whole garden back exactly where it was?**

## S01E07 — Finn's Perfect Shortcut

### Primary learning goals

1. **Engineering reasoning:** test, identify side effects, and revise a design.
2. **Systems/perspective reasoning:** evaluate a solution for multiple users and functions.

### Secondary learning

Spatial fit and measurement/comparison as required by the eventual interaction.

### Revisit / transfer role

Transfers E03 user constraints and E06 drainage/site reasoning into one engineering problem.

This is the major Apply/Understand episode for Thread D.

### EFL language functions

- describe a problem: too narrow / blocks / gets wet
- compare attempts: first / now / better
- propose revision: move / change / try another way
- give reasons

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- if a prototype performs its intended function, the design is successful;
- side effects are separate problems and need not affect the design;
- a clever solution deserves to stay because effort was spent building it.

### Evidence of thinking

The child identifies why the first design should change and selects/proposes a revision that addresses more than one constraint.

### Parent discussion opportunity

**It works for us. Who else uses this place, and what happens for them?**

## S01E08 — When the Steps Light Up

### Primary learning goals

1. **Reasoning:** test a visual pattern from more than one viewpoint before accepting it.
2. **Spatial/scientific reasoning:** reason about position, viewpoint, and alignment.

### Secondary learning

Efficient testing: gather the evidence needed without overbuilding.

### Revisit / transfer role

Revisits Thread A in a visually different context.

Reuses spatial reasoning without route-planning mechanics.

### EFL language functions

- position: from here / from there / higher / lower
- prediction: “If we move here…”
- checking: again / still / lines up
- uncertainty: looks like / maybe

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- one striking alignment proves intentional connection;
- if something looks aligned from one place, it must align everywhere;
- more lights or more building automatically make the test better.

### Evidence of thinking

The child checks the pattern from another relevant position and uses the result to strengthen or weaken the hypothesis.

### Parent discussion opportunity

**How could we check whether that pattern is real and not just something we see from here?**

## S01E09 — The Closed Hill

### Primary learning goals

1. **Reasoning/safety:** investigate within a real boundary and distinguish known from unknown.
2. **Spatial design:** find an alternate route that respects terrain and access constraints.

### Secondary learning

Terrain/erosion cause-and-effect at a high level.

### Revisit / transfer role

Transfers:

- E03 route constraints;
- E06 terrain reasoning;
- E08 evidence testing.

Deepens the idea that a constraint may change the solution rather than need to be removed.

### EFL language functions

- can / can't / need to
- safe / unsafe
- another way
- because
- “We know…” / “We don't know yet…”

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- every blocked path in a game should have a clever bypass;
- progress requires reaching the original location directly;
- uncertainty means failure.

### Evidence of thinking

The child chooses a safe information-gathering or alternate-route strategy and can distinguish evidence from unanswered questions.

### Parent discussion opportunity

**What can we still learn without going onto the closed path?**

## S01E10 — The Place with No Name

### Primary learning goals

1. **Humanities reasoning:** infer past uses of a place from multiple kinds of evidence.
2. **Perspective/community reasoning:** identify different current users' needs before choosing a new purpose.

### Secondary learning

Distinguish evidence of past use from preference about future use.

### Revisit / transfer role

Transfers E04 source comparison into a physical place.

Transfers Threads D/E from routes and engineering into community-space design.

### EFL language functions

- “This could have been…”
- used for / needed for
- “I would like…”
- “They need…”
- asking and comparing preferences

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- a place must have one single “correct” historic use;
- discovering the old purpose tells us automatically what the future purpose should be;
- the first or loudest preference represents everyone.

### Evidence of thinking

The child uses different clues to infer more than one plausible past use and considers more than one current need.

### Parent discussion opportunity

**What clues show that different people may have used this place in different ways?**

## S01E11 — A New Line on the Map

### Primary learning goals

1. **Transfer/synthesis:** design a workable route and shared-space plan using multiple constraints and earlier evidence.
2. **Reasoning/communication:** justify a choice, compare alternatives, and revise when a tradeoff appears.

### Secondary learning

Represent past and present change clearly in the Chronicle/map.

### Revisit / transfer role

Season-wide Deepen episode.

Expected transfers include:

- observation/evidence from E01–E08;
- route constraints from E03;
- chronology/change from E04–E06;
- design iteration from E07;
- boundaries/alternatives from E09;
- user needs from E10.

### EFL language functions

- proposing: “We could…”
- comparing alternatives
- simple agreement/disagreement
- reasons and tradeoffs
- retelling how the plan changed
- “We changed it because…”

### Likely misconceptions or incomplete models

- the best final plan must satisfy every preference fully;
- compromise means nobody got a good result;
- the old route should determine the new route;
- a final design should not need revision.

### Evidence of thinking

The child can make or support a plan using multiple constraints, cite earlier evidence/experience, and revise at least one element when a conflict appears.

### Parent discussion opportunity

**Which earlier discovery changed what you would build or choose now?**

# Load and variety audit

## Primary-goal load

Each episode has no more than two primary goals.

No episode is required to cover English, mathematics, science, and humanities simultaneously.

## Cognitive variety

Across the season, the dominant thinking shifts among:

- observe;
- compare;
- plan;
- sequence;
- infer;
- test;
- revise;
- take perspective;
- synthesize.

## Mathematics is present without forced arithmetic

Season 1 naturally supports:

- spatial reasoning;
- route comparison;
- informal estimation;
- measurement;
- constraint fitting.

It does not currently need arbitrary arithmetic problems. Exact quantitative content should be added only when the episode interaction genuinely needs it.

## Science/engineering is reasoning-first

The season emphasizes:

- observation;
- evidence;
- testing;
- cause/effect;
- systems;
- iteration.

It should not become a sequence of fact-recall science lessons.

## Humanities is evidence- and perspective-based

History/community learning emerges through:

- chronology;
- changing places;
- memories and records;
- preservation/adaptation;
- shared-space needs.

Avoid a single authoritative adult narrator explaining “the history lesson.”

## EFL recurrence without drill

Language functions recur because the trio repeatedly needs to:

- notice;
- compare;
- explain;
- navigate;
- test;
- negotiate.

Do not force vocabulary repetition when the story does not need the word.

# Parent-guidance rules for Season 1

This document identifies **discussion opportunities**, not final cue copy.

During later Episode Production Brief work:

- most episodes should use only a few sparse cues;
- the child normally notices/predicts/tries before the adult is prompted;
- parent prompts should not reveal evidence the child has not noticed;
- prompts should be skippable unless a shared activity genuinely requires two people;
- one strong cue may be better than several weak interruptions;
- parent participation should degrade gracefully if the adult briefly steps away.

# Misconception policy

The listed misconceptions are design hypotheses, not labels applied to children.

They may inform:

- world feedback;
- optional hints;
- parent prompts;
- later callbacks.

Unexpected answers should not automatically be treated as errors.

# Learning evidence policy

Do not reduce Season 1 to scored answers.

Useful evidence includes whether the child:

- notices a relevant feature;
- changes a route for a real constraint;
- sequences evidence meaningfully;
- proposes a test;
- revises after feedback;
- distinguishes known from unknown;
- considers another person's need;
- transfers an earlier idea into the final design.

The Episode Production Brief should later specify which evidence is observable in each interaction and what world feedback follows.

# Stage 3 exit-condition check

The learning weave satisfies the Stage 3 exit condition:

- learning reinforces the locked story rather than replacing it;
- primary learning load is restrained;
- important ideas recur in meaningfully different contexts;
- multiple threads reach transfer/deepening in E09–E11;
- EFL language functions support cognitively age-appropriate reasoning;
- parent discussion opportunities elicit thinking rather than answers.

Season 1 is ready to proceed to **Stage 4 — Episode Production Specification**, without beginning E01 production yet.
