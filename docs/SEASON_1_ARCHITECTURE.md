# Season 1 Story Architecture

## Status

Approved story architecture for Season 1.

This locks the narrative skeleton only. It does not lock full scripts, interaction mechanics, learning objectives, EFL load, parent cues, art, audio, or implementation.

Working internal label: **The Missing Place**. The final child-facing season title remains open.

## Central premise and mystery

During an ordinary repair of the Town Square noticeboard, Milo, Nia, and Finn discover part of an older Wondervale map hidden beneath a newer layer.

A faded route crosses familiar parts of town, then continues toward a place that is blank on current maps. Small old route markers still survive, but different residents remember the route differently.

Milo first treats it like a treasure trail. The real mystery becomes:

- Where did the old route go?
- Why do maps and memories disagree?
- What was the missing place?
- Should the old route be restored exactly, or should Wondervale make something that fits the town now?

The answer is non-villainous. The route faded through accumulated change: Market Lane shifted, a garden moved, the old station closed, and a North Hill section became unsafe after erosion. The final major break was the hill closure.

The blank place is an old flexible shared courtyard, internally called **the Common**. It once supported ordinary gatherings, games, notices, repair days, meals, and small performances rather than hiding a treasure.

The finale reopens the Common safely and creates a **new loop** through present-day Wondervale instead of recreating the past exactly.

Season idea:

> A place can remember its past without becoming trapped in it.

## Why this replaces The Lost River

The Lost River has a strong hook, but it pulls a whole first season toward one environmental problem and repeated escalation around water, cause-finding, and repair.

The Missing Place is stronger for Season 1 because it:

- makes Wondervale itself a continuing character;
- supports exploration, history, maps, building, science, social reasoning, and communication without making one subject dominate;
- creates gradual visible changes across recurring locations;
- pressures all three core characters differently;
- supports funny, quiet, reflective, atmospheric, and active episodes;
- rewards callbacks without requiring constant danger;
- ends by adding a permanent place and route to the world.

The Lost River is therefore not Season 1 canon, but remains available as a future story seed.

## Starting state of Wondervale

At the start:

- Town Square is active and familiar.
- The Riverside is usable but includes a dead-end/overlook that no longer connects as old maps suggest.
- Market Lane is busy, with imperfect wayfinding.
- The old station still exists but its public foyer is closed.
- The current community garden is uphill from where older maps place it.
- Lantern Steps still connect two levels of town, but the old lighting is mostly inactive.
- A North Hill route is fenced because an older segment is unsafe.
- The Common is not part of current everyday life and is unclear on the current town map.
- Milo, Nia, and Finn are established friends but still rely strongly on their default problem-solving styles.
- The Wondervale Chronicle has little Season 1 history; the season gives it its first major connected chain of memories.

Wondervale should feel lived-in, not broken. The old route is a mystery inside a functioning town.

## End-of-season changed state

By the end:

- the old route is understood as something that changed over time, not one perfect original path;
- the Riverside overlook is safely usable;
- Market Lane has clearer wayfinding;
- the old station foyer has become a small recurring Map Room / work area rather than returning to its old transport role;
- a lower-garden pocket reconnects the memory of the old garden site with the current garden;
- a redesigned connector path remains available;
- Lantern Steps are relit;
- a safe North Hill alternate path/overlook is marked while the genuinely unsafe old segment remains closed;
- the Common is reopened for present-day use;
- a new Wondervale loop connects several of these places;
- the Chronicle records old/new maps, major discoveries, and before/after world changes;
- the trio has visibly changed how they investigate, test, communicate, and design together.

The season leaves Wondervale more connected, not dramatically transformed.

# Story Arcs

## Arc 1 — A Map That Does Not Match (E01–E04)

Purpose:

- establish the trio, town, Chronicle, and mystery;
- prove the old map is real but incomplete;
- prevent the season from becoming a simple treasure hunt;
- establish that maps, memories, and places can all change.

Payoff:

Different residents are remembering genuine versions of a route that evolved over time. There was no single fixed route.

## Arc 2 — A Town That Changed (E05–E08)

Purpose:

- discover why the route changed;
- create several small persistent world changes;
- push Nia to act with incomplete information;
- give Finn a solution that technically works but is wrong for the wider situation;
- move Milo from quick explanation toward deliberate testing.

Payoff:

The route was not lost at once. Wondervale accumulated changes, and North Hill closure was the final major break in the full loop.

## Arc 3 — Make a New Way (E09–E11)

Purpose:

- treat a real safety boundary as a boundary, not a puzzle to bypass;
- reveal the missing place;
- turn historical reconstruction into a present-day community choice;
- pay off the trio's growth through one shared design problem.

Payoff:

The Common reopens and Wondervale gains a new loop that deliberately differs from the old one.

# Core Episodes

Episode titles are working titles. Narrative functions are locked; detailed scenes and final titles remain open.

## S01E01 — The Map Behind the Board

- **Story purpose:** hook the child with a visual mystery and establish the trio's different instincts.
- **Central problem:** an older map layer shows a route toward a blank current-map area. Is it real, mistaken, or something else?
- **Character beat:** Milo wants to follow it immediately; Nia records what is actually visible; Finn notices physical mounting evidence and a surviving route plate. Their approaches combine.
- **World change/discovery:** the first old marker is cleaned and visible again; the map fragment becomes the Chronicle's first major Season 1 mystery.
- **Setup/payoff:** sets up the blank-place symbol, old markers, evidence versus explanation, and the Chronicle as memory. The symbol pays off in E10.
- **Reason to continue:** the first marker points toward a Riverside connection that now appears to go nowhere.

## S01E02 — The Bridge That Stops

- **Story purpose:** make the first interactive exploration prove that the old map cannot be followed literally.
- **Central problem:** an old Riverside connection ends at a newer barrier and changed edge. The trio must infer what happened without bypassing the barrier.
- **Character beat:** Milo first assumes one neat cause; physical evidence forces a correction.
- **World change/discovery:** safe side access to a Riverside overlook is opened; the old route is confirmed as real but no longer geographically/currently exact.
- **Setup/payoff:** pays off E01 and introduces the idea that the shortest old path may not be today's usable path.
- **Reason to continue:** a surviving mark points toward busy Market Lane.

## S01E03 — Three Deliveries, One Wrong Turn

- **Story purpose:** break the clue-hunt rhythm with a fast, practical, lightly comic town problem.
- **Central problem:** a Market Lane obstruction confuses three small deliveries that cannot all use the same stairs, turns, or narrow passage.
- **Character beat:** Finn's shortest route works for the trio but not for one cart: the first strong “works for whom?” beat.
- **World change/discovery:** clearer wayfinding is added to Market Lane; another old route marker is found in the current streetscape.
- **Setup/payoff:** calls forward to Finn's E07 redesign. Two residents remember the old route differently: station versus garden.
- **Reason to continue:** believable memories conflict, so arrows alone can no longer solve the mystery.

## S01E04 — Everyone Remembers It Differently

- **Story purpose:** finish Arc 1 by shifting the question from “Where does the line go?” to “Which version of Wondervale are we seeing?”
- **Central problem:** memories, photographs, route fragments, and maps disagree.
- **Character beat:** Nia's records become essential but cannot create perfect certainty; Milo holds more than one possible explanation; Finn turns evidence into a timeline.
- **World change/discovery:** the Chronicle gains an old-route timeline; both conflicting residents are shown to remember genuine periods.
- **Setup/payoff:** pays off E03 and establishes the station and moved garden as Arc 2 anchors.
- **Reason to continue:** if the route changed, the trio wants to discover why.

## S01E05 — The Station with No Trains

- **Story purpose:** open Arc 2 with place-based inference and adaptive reuse.
- **Central problem:** inside the unused public station foyer, the trio must infer how spaces, objects, and an old route board were used instead of receiving a history lecture.
- **Character beat:** Nia chooses a small reversible test despite incomplete information; it gives partial evidence rather than a complete answer.
- **World change/discovery:** the foyer remains open as a small Map Room/work area. Records confirm route changes, a garden detour, and that full-loop use stopped around the North Hill closure.
- **Setup/payoff:** pays off E04 and sets up the moved garden and hill history.
- **Reason to continue:** how can a whole garden be in the “wrong” place on an old map?

## S01E06 — The Garden That Moved

- **Story purpose:** show that change can preserve continuity rather than represent damage.
- **Central problem:** the current garden is healthy but uphill from the earliest map. The trio investigates why it moved and what should happen at the lower old site.
- **Character beat:** Milo initially likes the neat idea of putting the garden back; the trio learns the move solved real problems and the living garden continued.
- **World change/discovery:** a small lower-garden pocket is started and can visibly mature later. Drainage and terrain become meaningful evidence.
- **Setup/payoff:** pays off the station record; drainage returns in E07/E09; the planting pays off visually in E11.
- **Reason to continue:** Finn sees a way to reconnect current locations with a new shortcut.

## S01E07 — Finn's Perfect Shortcut

- **Story purpose:** deliver Finn's clearest failure-and-revision episode.
- **Central problem:** Finn builds/designs a clever connector that works for the trio but interferes with another user's route and with drainage or routine access.
- **Character beat:** Finn's idea is genuinely clever; his growth is accepting that “it works” is an incomplete test and redesigning it.
- **World change/discovery:** a revised connector path remains and reaches the base of Lantern Steps.
- **Setup/payoff:** callbacks to E03 delivery constraints and E06 drainage; sets up the Steps.
- **Reason to continue:** an evening lighting test may reveal where the upper old route went.

## S01E08 — When the Steps Light Up

- **Story purpose:** provide atmosphere, wonder, and a quieter visual discovery before the final arc.
- **Central problem:** only part of the old Lantern Steps lighting can be restored for evening use. From the right viewpoints, old brackets and markers align toward North Hill.
- **Character beat:** Milo notices the pattern, then tests it from another position instead of declaring victory; Nia supports action on a testable idea; Finn resists overbuilding.
- **World change/discovery:** Lantern Steps are persistently relit; a maintenance/date clue connects them to the hill closure period.
- **Setup/payoff:** pays off E07 and points several independent clues toward North Hill.
- **Reason to continue:** the most convincing direction is visibly fenced and closed.

## S01E09 — The Closed Hill

- **Story purpose:** prove that not every barrier exists for the child to defeat.
- **Central problem:** the old hill segment is unsafe and stays closed. The trio must observe safely, understand why, and find a present-day alternate route.
- **Character beat:** Nia leads a bounded survey despite unresolved uncertainty; Milo accepts “we cannot go there” as information; Finn does not invent a risky bypass.
- **World change/discovery:** a safe alternate hill path/overlook is marked; the unsafe segment remains closed. From the overlook the trio sees a marker/wall matching the blank-place symbol.
- **Setup/payoff:** callbacks to E06 terrain and E08 alignment; explains the final historical break in the loop.
- **Reason to continue:** the E01 symbol is finally tied to a real reachable place.

## S01E10 — The Place with No Name

- **Story purpose:** reveal the mystery without a treasure-chest payoff.
- **Central problem:** an overgrown but usable courtyard contains traces of many former community uses. The new question is not “what treasure is here?” but “what should this place be now?”
- **Character beat:** Finn reframes restoration as present-day function; Nia gathers multiple perspectives; Milo resists committing to the first exciting use.
- **World change/discovery:** the Common becomes visitable/understandable but remains intentionally unfinished. The blank symbol is explained.
- **Setup/payoff:** pays off E01, E04, E05, and E08–09. The old loop's gradual disappearance is fully understood.
- **Reason to continue:** the child now helps decide what a permanent part of Wondervale becomes next.

## S01E11 — A New Line on the Map

- **Story purpose:** pay off the season through creation, callbacks, and visible character growth rather than a larger threat.
- **Central problem:** the Common cannot contain every idea, and the new route cannot simply trace the old one. The trio and residents must choose a workable layout and current route under real constraints.
- **Character beat:** Milo asks for evidence/tests before fixing an idea; Nia proposes a reversible layout trial; Finn checks users, side effects, and purpose before calling the design finished.
- **World change/discovery:** the Common opens; a new Wondervale loop is added; Riverside access, Market signs, Station Map Room, growing lower garden, connector, Lantern Steps, and hill overlook remain visible. The Chronicle gains an old/new map spread.
- **Setup/payoff:** functions as the main season callback engine and integrates the mystery, world growth, practical constraints, and trio arcs.
- **Reason to continue:** the reward is a Wondervale with new places and future possibilities, not an emergency cliffhanger.

# Core trio growth

## Milo

Start: acts quickly and often treats the first plausible explanation as enough reason to proceed.

Progression: E02 correction -> E04 holds multiple explanations -> E08 tests a pattern -> E09 respects a boundary -> E11 naturally gathers evidence before committing.

End: still the most action-oriented, but “let's try” increasingly means a purposeful test.

## Nia

Start: observes and communicates well but prefers to reduce uncertainty before action.

Progression: E01–04 records make the mystery intelligible -> E05 reversible test -> E06 accepts partial/overlapping perspectives -> E09 leads bounded investigation -> E11 prototypes before perfect agreement.

End: remains careful, but acts more readily when the next step is safe, reversible, and informative.

## Finn

Start: overvalues cleverness, efficiency, and “it works.”

Progression: E03 shortest route fails a real user -> E07 major redesign -> E09 refuses unsafe cleverness -> E10–11 evaluates users, side effects, fairness, and purpose.

End: remains inventive and playful, but “who does this work for?” is now part of building.

## Trio relationship

The season does not prove one approach correct. Their instincts move from competing toward being sequenced and combined:

notice -> propose -> test -> listen -> revise -> build.

# Recurring locations

- **Town Square:** home base, first discovery, public information; old marker becomes visible again.
- **Riverside overlook:** first proof old maps and current reality diverge; safe access opens.
- **Market Lane:** humor and practical constraints; wayfinding improves.
- **Old Station / Map Room:** traces of the past and adaptive reuse; foyer gains a new limited function.
- **Current garden + lower garden pocket:** continuity through change; visible growth across episodes.
- **Lantern Steps:** evening atmosphere and visual discovery; lights return.
- **North Hill:** real boundary and terrain history; alternate overlook opens while unsafe path stays closed.
- **The Common:** central payoff; reopened for present-day shared use.

# Recurring NPC roles

Names, visual identities, ages, and exact relationships remain open.

- Noticeboard/archive steward: gives access to incomplete records, not answers.
- Market vendor/delivery coordinator: has current practical goals and one route memory.
- Long-term resident/storykeeper: offers meaningful but imperfect memory.
- Station caretaker/maintenance worker: controls legitimate access and knows some systems/records without omniscience.
- Garden coordinator/neighbor: represents the living present of the garden.
- Town maintenance/safety role: sets legitimate North Hill limits and is not an antagonist.
- Community users: a small recurring mix of recognizable residents so “community” is not an anonymous crowd.

# Mystery, foreshadowing, callbacks, and payoff

## Mystery ladder

1. Faded line -> real former public route.
2. Conflicting memories -> route changed over time.
3. Broken loop -> several gradual changes; hill closure was the final major break.
4. Blank place -> the Common.
5. Restore old loop? -> no; create a new route that remembers the old while serving the present.

## Major callback chain

- E01 blank symbol -> E10 Common.
- E01 Chronicle evidence -> E04 timeline -> E11 old/new map spread.
- E02 unusable old link -> recurring rule that old is not automatically best.
- E03 delivery constraint -> E07 redesign -> E11 route planning.
- E04 conflicting memories -> E05/E06 proof both can be valid.
- E05 hill date -> E08 matching history -> E09 explanation.
- E06 drainage/terrain -> E07 side effect -> E09 reasoning.
- E06 planting -> visible E11 growth.
- E07 Steps access -> E08 atmospheric reveal.
- E08 alignment -> E09 safe hill investigation.
- E09 alternate route -> E10 Common access.
- E10 user perspectives -> E11 design.

## Final payoff guardrails

Do not use:

- a hidden villain;
- a magical master key;
- a reward chest;
- an adult exposition dump;
- exact restoration of every old feature;
- a compulsory Season 2 cliffhanger.

The strongest payoff is recognition: the child has seen enough pieces to understand the place before the characters finish explaining it.

# Natural learning doors — high level only

Detailed learning objectives belong to Stage 3 and are not locked here.

Natural openings include:

- maps, routes, direction, position, distance, scale, spatial constraints, and measurement;
- evidence versus explanation, reversible tests, light/viewpoint, drainage, terrain, erosion, plant growth, prototypes, revision, and side effects;
- place change over time, memories versus records, multiple perspectives, public-space purpose, preservation versus adaptation, fairness, and whose needs a design serves;
- EFL functions such as describing, comparing, directions, prediction, reasons, clarification, simple disagreement, explaining change, and retelling.

These are opportunities, not curriculum requirements for every episode.

# Variety, pacing, emotional range, and continuity audit

## Episode drivers

- E01 discovery mystery
- E02 physical exploration
- E03 practical/comic delivery problem
- E04 conflicting sources
- E05 place-based inference
- E06 reflective history/living environment
- E07 build-test-redesign
- E08 atmospheric light/viewpoint
- E09 safety/terrain investigation
- E10 reveal plus perspective-taking
- E11 collaborative creation and celebration

The season therefore does not default to “find clue -> solve puzzle -> get next clue.”

## Emotional range

Curiosity, humor, frustration, uncertainty, surprise, reflection, satisfaction after revision, evening wonder, disappointment at a real limit, discovery, and celebration all appear.

No episode needs a larger threat than the previous episode.

## World-growth pacing

Persistent changes are distributed across the season and are usually small: access, signs, a reopened room, planting, connector, lights, overlook. The Common and new loop are largest because they integrate earlier work.

## Character pacing

Each core character has an early tendency signal, a meaningful correction/failure, and later chances to show changed behavior without announcing the lesson.

## Continuity and return-after-absence

Callbacks can be visually or verbally reintroduced. Progress must not depend on remembering a code, phrase, or obscure clue from many episodes earlier.

## Formula guardrails

Do not repeatedly:

- open every episode with a mystery object;
- end every episode with an arrow to the next place;
- make all three characters restate their personality roles;
- have an adult praise the “lesson”;
- make every world change a repair;
- use building whenever Finn matters;
- use conversation whenever Nia matters.

# Canonical architecture decisions

Locked:

- Missing Place / old-route mystery replaces The Lost River for Season 1.
- 11 Core Episodes in 3 Story Arcs.
- The old route changed over time; conflicting maps/memories can all be valid.
- No villain deliberately erased the route.
- North Hill closure is the final major historical break.
- The unsafe old hill segment remains closed at season end.
- The final blank place is a former shared community space, internally called the Common.
- The Common is reopened for present-day use rather than restored exactly.
- Wondervale receives a new loop at season end.
- Earlier episode world changes remain visible later.
- Milo, Nia, and Finn reach the growth endpoints defined in CORE_TRIO.md through the beats above.
- Season 1 resolves without requiring a Season 2 cliffhanger.
