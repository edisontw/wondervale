# Product Vision

## What Wondervale is

Wondervale is a parent-guided interactive learning world for children ages 6–9, designed **EFL-first**.

The child directly controls a recurring protagonist and explores alongside a small core cast. The child should feel that they are entering a living story world, not opening a school exercise site. The adult should feel useful without needing teaching expertise.

## Core experience

Wondervale combines a **storybook shell** with **living interactive scenes**.

A typical session is 12–20 minutes:

1. A story problem creates a reason to care.
2. The child enters a persistent location and controls the protagonist.
3. The child explores before being told the answer.
4. A short parent cue invites prediction, explanation, comparison, or connection when useful.
5. The child acts: build, test, sort, search, measure, read, calculate, choose, or discuss.
6. The world responds visibly.
7. The story advances and may permanently change a location or character relationship.
8. The Wondervale Chronicle records a meaningful event, discovery, or question.
9. A concept may return later in a different context.

## Product pillars

### Story creates motivation
Knowledge should solve problems that matter inside the story.

### Direct agency through a real protagonist
The child controls one recurring protagonist with an authored personality and growth arc. Agency comes from meaningful action and strategy, not from a blank avatar.

### Parent-guided, child-driven
Parent prompts should create conversation, not turn the adult into a lecturer.

### EFL language, age-appropriate thinking
Simple English must not imply simple thinking. Language support and cognitive challenge are separate design dimensions.

### Persistent continuity
Wondervale changes over time. A shop opened in Episode 18 should still exist later. A character who learned to test a hypothesis should remember that growth.

### Learning through transfer
A skill learned in one context should reappear in another. The goal is not completion of isolated lessons but increasingly flexible use of knowledge.

### Variety without chaos
Activities should vary in surface form and cognitive demand while still using a manageable reusable engine underneath.

### Gentle challenge
Failure should produce useful consequences, clues, humor, or another attempt rather than punishment.

### Diegetic reward
The main reward is seeing the story, characters, and world grow. Scores, streaks, and currencies are not the core motivation.

## Initial subject scope

The first version may draw from:

- English language and literacy;
- mathematics;
- science and engineering thinking;
- humanities, culture, geography, and social reasoning.

These are internal content dimensions, not necessarily top-level child navigation.

## Core cast direction

Begin with a trio:

- one player-controlled protagonist;
- two recurring companions;
- additional characters introduced gradually.

They should represent different personalities and approaches to problems, not school subjects.

## What Wondervale should avoid

- worksheet gameplay with decorative stories;
- repeated multiple-choice loops;
- EFL content reduced to vocabulary drills;
- reward systems that replace intrinsic curiosity;
- heavy parent instructions during play;
- characters who exist only to teach one subject;
- a huge open world before the content model is proven;
- resetting the world after every episode;
- collecting child data before it is genuinely needed.

## Initial product test

The first vertical slice succeeds if:

- the child understands enough English to act without having every line translated;
- the child cares about what happens to the protagonist and companions;
- the child wants to know what happens next;
- the parent naturally talks with the child;
- the activity requires thinking rather than guessing the UI;
- the child can recover from mistakes;
- the world feels changed by the episode;
- the family would voluntarily return for another episode.
